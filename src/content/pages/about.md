## Where the poems come from

Most of Bita's poems begin as something overheard or half seen: a word on a shop sign, a hinge, the way a stranger holds a cup with both hands. She keeps a notebook small enough for a coat pocket, and most of what is on this site started in one.

She is drawn to rooms and what they remember, to weather and what it changes, and to the distance between people that language tries, and usually fails, to cross. Her poems tend to be quiet. They are not shy.

## How she works

She writes by hand first and types later, so every piece here has been written at least twice. Drafts are read aloud, cut, and read aloud again. If a line survives being said out loud on a night bus, it stays.

She writes poems, prose poems, short essays about the craft, and fragments: the pieces that refuse to become anything longer, and are better for it.

## This site

This is where she keeps finished things, and a few unfinished ones. Every piece has a margin. If a line stays with you, leave a note there: a word, a question, the day you read it.
