Dear —

I found your handwriting
on the inside of the cupboard door:
a ladder of heights in pencil,
four names climbing toward the ceiling,
the last one stopping
        just below the hinge.

I haven't painted over it.
I wanted you to know that.
I open the door for the flour
and there you are, measuring.

Your roses came up again this year,
too many, unruly, the colour of a mouth
after wine. I don't know their names.
I call them yours.

The neighbours say you moved to the sea.
I like to think the light there
is the kind that forgives a window.
I like to think you sleep.

Some nights the house still listens
for your weight on the fourth stair.
I step on it for you.
I let it speak.

Yours, from your kitchen,
        which is mine now,
                which was never
                        either of ours —

the new woman
